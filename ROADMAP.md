# Roadmap

BiasLens shows how an article argues: who speaks, which claims rest on what, and
which techniques shape the reader's conclusion. Then it lets a reader send a
reply worth reading. The build plan behind these items is
`docs/plans/2026-10-report-v2.md`. It carries no delivery dates; a checked
milestone means the capability is implemented and verified, not merely proposed.

## Now

### A report worth reading

Every finding points to a passage in the article, and every voice is named with
who they are and whether they support the main claim.

- [ ] A test set of real Swiss and English articles, scored before and after every method change
- [x] A published catalogue of writing techniques, each with what counts and what does not
- [x] Quotes found even when a PDF or a copy-paste changed the spacing, hyphens or quotation marks
- [x] Articles read from PDF links and uploaded PDFs, including press clippings
- [x] Claims attributed to the person who made them, not to the article
- [x] Figures checked for a source, a sample size and contradictions within the article
- [x] The article shown with its passages highlighted, one tap to see why
- [x] The report in the reader's language, including Swiss German

## Next

### Replies you can send

Someone sends you an article. You send back BiasLens's reading of it, as a
one-line message, an image, a short brief, a full page or a document, and the
person who opens it sees something they had not noticed within ten seconds.

- [x] Saved reports with their own link
- [x] A reply page made for the person receiving it
- [x] Reply lengths for every occasion: one line, a short brief, a full page
- [ ] A printable dossier of a reading, for classes and letters to the editor
- [x] Share images for messages, posts and stories
- [x] Open any article by putting the BiasLens address in front of its link, or from the phone's share menu

### Who is speaking

- [x] Profiles of authors, quoted people and outlets from sourced public records, with a source on every line
- [ ] What BiasLens reports on an author's or outlet's articles show, with the number of articles and its limits stated

### Rewrite studio

- [x] A neutral version of the article that fixes the flagged passages
- [ ] Each change in the neutral version linked to the passage it fixes
- [x] A response to the article: from BiasLens, as a letter to the editor, or as a draft of your own
- [x] The same facts written from stated values (the eight smartvote dimensions) instead of a political label
- [x] "How would this outlet write it", always marked as a simulation
- [x] "How would this public figure answer it", only for people with a public body of work, always marked as a simulation
- [x] A check that a rewrite kept the original facts and added none

### Investigations you can return to

Preserve the path from a question and source through claims, evidence and
uncertainty, while keeping model interpretation distinct from independent
verification.

- [ ] Save, revisit and revise investigations across sessions
- [ ] Independent source retrieval with traceable citations
- [ ] Compare claims across sources without collapsing disagreement into a single truth or bias score

## Later

### Conversation analysis you can inspect

The transcript tools keep their limits: an overlap is not proof of an
interruption, and a speaker label is not independently verified.

- [ ] Reviewable source timestamps and correction history on conversation records
- [ ] Audio and video transcription with editable speaker attribution, evaluated before any comparison is shown

### Reasons to come back

- [ ] Several outlets' coverage of one story, compared side by side
- [ ] A daily "spot the technique" round with a shareable result
- [ ] Replies that continue as a conversation between the two people
- [ ] Your reports and replies kept in your account

### An open method you can challenge

- [ ] "Disagree with this" on every finding, with corrections shown on the report
- [ ] Published accuracy per technique and per method version
- [ ] Moderated discussion and challenge resolution with a visible record of what changed and why
- [ ] Reproducible records of which model and provider produced a report, without implying that availability means quality

### BiasLens as a publication

- [ ] Regular published readings of the stories people are sending each other
- [ ] Labelled debates: two simulated outlets answer the same facts
- [ ] A newsletter, and audio and video versions of readings
- [ ] An interface for newsrooms and teachers
