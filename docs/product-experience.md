# Experience and audience hypotheses

This is a working product/validation brief, not a replacement for the product
mission. The canonical mission remains in Loki; planned work and shipped
changes live in this repository's `ROADMAP.md` and `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Design assessment

The editorial typography and restrained palette support careful reading. They
are not yet a distinctive daily investigation experience. Keep the readable
foundation, introduce an interactive question demonstration, and orient the
application around work someone can continue. Repeat use should come from useful
saved investigations, changing evidence and collaboration, rather than badges,
a truth score, urgency or a feed engineered for outrage.

The entry points are a source, a question and a timed conversation. Public
method/development documents support those journeys; they should not displace
tools in the main navigation. A private profile/security page manages identity.
Do not imply a public expert profile, reputation score or cross-device storage
exists when it does not. The current notebook is browser-local and exportable.

## Who to validate first

Start with people who repeatedly research an argument and need to show their
work: independent analysts, specialist writers, researchers and investigative
journalists. Finance, science and policy are subjects, not separate products.
These users can judge source traceability, alternative explanations and exports.
Professional scientific work requires stronger retrieval, methods and reference
handling than the beta offers; this is not a literature-review replacement.

Educators and students are a useful second group for guided reasoning and
conversation-analysis exercises. Curious general readers are a broad audience
but have less reason to pay or return before the core workflow saves time.
Teams conducting interviews, panels, earnings calls or debates are a separate
conversation-analysis workflow, not evidence of left/right bias.

These are hypotheses, not customer research. Validate with small task-based
sessions: bring a real source, find a consequential assumption, inspect its
quotation, record a rival explanation and return to the question later. Record
where a person cannot continue without help. Ask which current tool or manual
step it replaces. Do not infer demand from compliments or sign-ups alone.

## Other product facets to examine

- Source examination across news, science, finance and other fields: faithful
  quotation, claim scope, missing context, counterevidence and model uncertainty.
- Question investigations: multiple saved questions, import/export, provenance,
  comparisons and evidence changes. Browser-local single-notebook continuity is
  available; cloud history and collaboration remain absent.
- Conversation analysis: timestamped speaking time and overlap are available.
  An overlap is not automatically an interruption; intent, turn-taking and
  moderation require reviewable context. Audio/video transcription, speaker
  correction, interruption annotation and question allocation need evaluation.
- Comparison and verification: cross-source coverage, primary-source retrieval,
  independent support, contradictions and adversarial review need their own
  quality gates. Model-generated interpretation is not external verification.

Potential repeat-use measures: unassisted task completion, grounded quotations,
material reasoning errors, time to an exportable useful finding, investigation
reopening after seven days, and whether users add evidence that changes their
view. Gather metrics with consent and avoid retaining article text or personal
research by default. No analytics service or paid infrastructure is required for
initial moderated sessions.
